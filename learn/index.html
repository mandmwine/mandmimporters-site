Serving guide

The right temperature

Temperature changes everything. Below 8°C aromas are muted; too warm and alcohol takes over. As a rule, the more tannic the wine the warmer it is served, the fruitier the cooler.

StyleCelsiusFahrenheitExamples
Sparkling and Champagne6 to 8°C43 to 46°FStraight from the ice bucket
Crisp whites and rosé8 to 10°C46 to 50°FSancerre, Greco, Provence rosé
Rich whites10 to 13°C50 to 55°FBurgundy, Pessac-Léognan blanc
Light reds13 to 15°C55 to 59°FPinot Noir, Frappato; a slight chill helps
Medium reds15 to 17°C59 to 63°FChianti, Barbera, Merlot
Full reds17 to 18°C63 to 65°FBordeaux, Barolo, Brunello, Aglianico

To decant or not

Decant young, powerful reds such as Bordeaux, Barolo and Aglianico for an hour or two to let them open. Decant older reds only to remove sediment, and pour gently: their bouquet is delicate and can fade with too much air. Whites and sparkling wines rarely need it.
